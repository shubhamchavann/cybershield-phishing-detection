LOOKALIKE DOMAINS

Typosquatting & Domain Spoofing

Scammers register domains that closely resemble genuine brands (e.g. sbi-rewards.xyz or amaz0n-security.com). They replicate the official login page pixel-by-pixel to intercept your credentials.

⚠️ Rule: Always inspect the domain suffix before typing passwords.
⚡
FEAR COERCION

Electricity & Utility Bill Threats

Attackers send mass SMS alerts warning that your power or SIM card will be cut tonight unless you call an "officer" or download a bill-updating APK that installs device screen-sharing software.

⛔ Rule: Utility companies never cut connections via personal phone numbers.
💸
PAYMENT TRAPS

UPI & QR Code Reverse Scams

A fraudster posing as a buyer sends a QR code or "payment link", insisting you must scan it or enter your UPI PIN to "receive payment". In reality, entering your PIN authorizes an outflow of funds.

⛔ Rule: You NEVER need to enter a UPI PIN to receive money.
💼
TASK FRAUD

Part-Time Job & Telegram Task Scams

Victims are offered Rs. 150-300 for liking hotel reviews or YouTube videos. After small payouts gain trust, victims are coerced into paying thousands for "VIP crypto tasks" and then blocked.

⚠️ Rule: Any job requiring you to deposit money first is a scam.
📦
MALWARE

Trojan APKs & Remote Access Tools

Malicious Android applications (.apk) disguised as banking reward portals, loan apps, or parcel tracking updates that secretly forward all incoming SMS OTPs directly to attacker command servers.

⛔ Rule: Only install apps from the official Google Play Store or Apple App Store.
🎁
LURE SCAMS

Lottery & Cashback Phishing

WhatsApp forwards promising free Rs. 500 mobile recharges, KBC lotteries, or festival subsidies requiring you to forward the link to 10 contacts and enter your banking credentials.

✔ Rule: If an offer sounds too good to be true, it is 100% fraudulent.
🛡️ DEBUNKING COMMON MYTHS

Cybersecurity Myths vs. Facts

MYTH: "The website has a green lock icon and HTTPS, so it must be 100% safe."
FACT: HTTPS only guarantees encryption in transit. Scammers easily get free SSL certificates for fake phishing websites.
MYTH: "Someone sent me money by asking me to scan their QR code."
FACT: QR codes are exclusively designed for PAYING, not receiving. Entering your PIN on a QR prompt always sends your money away.
MYTH: "The caller knew my name and account number, so they must be from my bank."
FACT: Attackers buy leaked databases on dark web forums and use basic personal info to establish fake credibility.
