Core Threat Classifications

The Attack: Scammers clone legitimate login portals (like Netflix, Microsoft 365, Google, or bank portals) to trick you into typing your email, password, or multi-factor authentication (MFA) codes.

🔍 Critical Red Flags:
  • Mismatched brand name in URL (e.g., https://netflix-payment-security.xyz).
  • Lack of HTTPS or SSL certificate warnings.
  • Generic greeting ("Dear customer" instead of your actual name).
  • The page asks for details normally not requested, such as security questions during a standard login.
💬 Real-World Scenario:
"Microsoft Account Security: We noticed unusual sign-in activity on your Outlook account from location 'Lagos, Nigeria'. To prevent account closure, verify your credentials immediately at: http://outlook-verify-login.xyz."

The Attack: Phishing sent via SMS. Since SMS messages are brief and read on-the-go, victims are more vulnerable. Often disguised as delivery updates, toll charges, or bank alerts.

🔍 Critical Red Flags:
  • Sent from a generic 10-digit mobile number instead of an official brand short-code.
  • High-urgency language ("your package will be returned to sender within 24 hours").
  • Uses suspicious URL shorteners (e.g., bit.ly, tinyurl.com, t.co) to mask the destination.
💬 Real-World Scenario:
"[USPS Alert]: Your package could not be delivered due to an incorrect house number. Please update your address and schedule a redelivery at: https://usps-redeliver-address.info/update."

The Attack: Scammers send QR codes via emails or paste physical stickers over legitimate QR codes (e.g., on parking meters or flyers). Scanning them bypasses email security filters, directing you to fraudulent web pages.

🔍 Critical Red Flags:
  • Physical stickers pasted over existing QR codes on parking meters or tables.
  • Unsolicited emails containing ONLY a QR code and urgent text asking you to scan with your phone.
  • Scanning takes you to a domain that doesn't match the expected service.
💬 Real-World Scenario:
An email claiming: "MFA Update Required: Scan this QR code immediately using your authenticator app to sync your company email account." The QR code redirects to a credential phishing site.

The Attack: Fraudsters display pop-ups on your computer claiming your device has a virus. They lock your browser and display toll-free numbers, trying to get you to call support and grant remote control.

🔍 Critical Red Flags:
  • Loud beeping sounds or flashing browser screens that won't let you close the window.
  • Urgent warnings from "Microsoft Security Essentials" or "Windows Defender" showing a random mobile phone number.
  • Support agents demanding remote control software (like AnyDesk or TeamViewer) to inspect your PC.
💬 Real-World Scenario:
"WINDOWS WARNING: Your system has detected a Trojan horse virus. To avoid data loss and identity theft, call Windows Support immediately at: +1-888-555-0199. Do not restart your computer."

🛡️ Unified Defense Rule

Phishing works by manipulating human emotions: Fear, Urgency, Curiosity, or Greed.

01
Inspect the Domain

Look at the exact URL spelling. Legitimate sites never use helper domains like paypal-secure.com.

02
Never Trust Senders

Email headers and sender addresses can be easily spoofed. Verify directly through official corporate apps or portals.

03
Slow Down

No legitimate institution will close your account, cancel a shipment, or demand payment within minutes of an email alert.

🚨 Think You're a Target?

If you typed credentials on a suspicious link scanned today:

  • Change the passwords of that account immediately.
  • Turn on Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA).
  • Notify your bank or organization immediately.
